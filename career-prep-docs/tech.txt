A practical “go deep” book list (ordered for fastest impact)
You’re already working in Java/Spring microservices + Kafka + Mongo, so I’m prioritizing books that directly map to what you’ll see in prod: reliability, event-driven flows, DDD boundaries, and system design tradeoffs.

Core architecture fundamentals (start here)
“Fundamentals of Software Architecture” (Richards & Ford)
Great modern overview: architectural styles, tradeoffs, fitness functions, how to reason about “good architecture”.
“Software Architecture: The Hard Parts” (Richards & Ford)
Practical decision-making: distributed data, decomposition, migrations, integration styles (very relevant to microservices/event-driven).
“Clean Architecture” (Robert C. Martin)
Not perfect, but very useful to internalize boundaries, dependency direction, and keeping business logic independent of frameworks.
“Building Evolutionary Architectures” (Ford, Parsons, Kua)
How to change architecture safely over time (key in real companies where systems evolve continuously).
Domain-Driven Design (DDD) (for boundaries, not just patterns)
“Domain-Driven Design” (Eric Evans)
The original. Read for concepts (ubiquitous language, bounded contexts, aggregates), not for code.
“Implementing Domain-Driven Design” (Vernon)
More actionable than Evans for building software; good “how to apply”.
“Domain-Driven Design Distilled” (Vernon)
Fast entry + refresher. If you’re new to DDD, start here before the big books.
“Learning Domain-Driven Design” (Vlad Khononov)
Excellent modern DDD with real-world constraints; very relevant for microservices and event-driven boundaries.
Why this matters for your work: most “Kafka mismatch” problems are actually unclear boundaries + weak invariants + missing end-to-end ownership more than “Kafka is unreliable”.

Event-driven architecture + messaging (where your current system lives)
“Designing Event-Driven Systems” (Ben Stopford)
Conceptual model of event streams, log-based thinking, and how to design event flows.
“Enterprise Integration Patterns” (Hohpe & Woolf)
Classic. Patterns like idempotency, retry, dead-letter, sagas, message channels—still the best “pattern vocabulary”.
“Microservices Patterns” (Chris Richardson)
Directly hits your world: sagas, outbox pattern, CQRS, integration patterns, and how to keep data consistent without distributed transactions.
Kafka specifically (deep, practical)
“Kafka: The Definitive Guide” (Narkhede, Shapira, Palino)
Best single Kafka book for producers/consumers/partitions/offsets/guarantees.
“Kafka in Action” (Duvall) (optional but very hands-on)
Good for building intuition by implementing.

producer acks/idempotence, retries, in-flight requests

Distributed systems + reliability (the “why did we lose events?” toolbox)
“Designing Data-Intensive Applications (DDIA)” (Martin Kleppmann)
Mandatory. It explains logs, streams, consistency, replication, transactions, and why distributed systems behave the way they do.
“Release It!” (Michael Nygard)
Production failure patterns + stability tactics (timeouts, bulkheads, circuit breakers, backpressure).
